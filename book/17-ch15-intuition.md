# Chapter 15

## Intuition: How and Why

Ayanokōji is famous for calculation. Calculation is real. It is also the part that photographs well.

Under it there is **intuition**, in Bergson’s sense: not a vibe, not a guess, not laziness about proof. Intuition as a precise attempt to coincide with a moving reality instead of cutting it into still pieces. The intellect is good at *how* — mechanisms, sequences, levers. Intuition is how a mind touches *why* a movement is this movement and not another.

The conversation, late, named a deeper layer: almost poetic, a mechanism that works with **intuitive reason**, a why instead of a how. This chapter takes that seriously, and then admits that he lives mostly on the lower floor of the same house.

### Two floors of the same faculty

**Floor one: practical intuition (how).**

He enters a room and knows, before a list exists, where the tension is. He hears a sentence and knows it is load-bearing. He feels a plan is wrong the way a musician feels a bad interval. Later he can justify. The justification is translation into intellect. The knowing was coincidence with the field’s current contraction.

This floor is why making-do works. The thin sample is enough if you can inhabit it as a living shape, not as three data points. Experts in any craft have this. His craft is human weather in a contest.

**Floor two: poetic intuition (why).**

This floor does not ask how to move the weather. It asks what the weather *is* — what the qualitative tone of the whole motion is, why this life has this flavor of emptiness and precision, why a particular person’s existence has a weight that is not a use-value. Meaning is not extracted as a conclusion. It is lived as the movement.

He rarely stays on floor two. Floor two does not win exams. It might win a life. He has not chosen a life in that sense. He has chosen not to be owned.

### Intuitive reason

The phrase sounds like a contradiction because we have been taught that reason is the cutting kind.

Intuitive reason is reason that stays with continuity. It is rigorous about not replacing a living motion with a diagram of the motion. It can still be exact. A poet is exact about tone. A musician is exact about time. He is exact about social forces. That exactness, when it is felt rather than tabulated, is already intuitive reason of the first floor.

The second floor would be exact about meaning. Why does Horikita’s pride hurt instead of only functioning? Why does a fake relationship produce a remainder? Why is freedom empty if it is only negative — freedom *from* — and what would freedom *for* even feel like? He can ask these as strategy questions (how would a human be motivated by them?). Asking them as why-questions would require coinciding with his own animation as something that might not be a tool.

That coincidence is dangerous to the White Room product. Products are not supposed to ask what they are for, except in the manufacturer’s voice.

### How versus why in a single incident

A classmate breaks.

How-intuition: the break was coming; here are the marks; here is the node; here is the smallest intervention that stabilizes the class.

Why-intuition: this break has a color — humiliation mixed with relief, say — and the color tells you what kind of world they were living in, and what kind of world would have to exist for this not to be the only possible color.

He is superb at the first. He can fake the second as language. Faking why is how you comfort someone on the way to using them. Real why would slow him down. He does not like being slowed except as tactic.

### Cultivating the second floor without his aims

The conversation wanted a way out of present-context captivity. Floor-one intuition already helps: you feel the larger motion, so the now is a wave, not the sea.

Floor-two intuition helps differently. It gives the motion a meaning that is not only a task. Without that, agency becomes the agency of a better tool. You choose more, and you are still only an operator.

A simple distinction to keep: if after a pause you have a better plan, you were on floor one. If after a pause the situation has a different *weight* — tragic, comic, tender, empty — and that weight is not a tactic, you brushed floor two.

He would call floor two inefficient. This book calls it the part of mind his legend leaves out, and therefore the part a reader must not skip if they want the mind rather than the brand.

### Intuition is not anti-thought

Thinking as one being includes intellect. Intuition is not a war against thinking. It is thinking in its continuous mode. He uses both: inhabit, then cut, then inhabit again. The cut makes a plan. The inhabit makes the plan touch reality.

When he fails, it is often because he cut too soon — a person reduced to a node too fast — or inhabited too little — a tone he did not feel, only inferred. The failures are human. They are also rare enough that the myth of perfection grows. The myth is an intellect-picture of him. The real mind is the oscillation.

### Poetry as mechanism

“Poetic in nature and meaning” does not mean he should speak in metaphors. It means the mechanism of the second floor is closer to how a poem works than how a machine works. A poem does not list a feeling. It makes a duration in which the feeling can be coincided with. His best conversations accidentally do this: a parallel, a contrast, a silence, and someone feels the shape of their own life. That is poetic mechanism used for a strategic why (to produce a conclusion). The same mechanism could be used to *dwell*.

He does not dwell. ### Why “almost poetic” is not an insult to him

Calling a layer poetic can sound like a demand that he become soft. It is not. Poetry, here, is exactness about qualitative unity. A surgeon can be poetic in this sense about a body without writing verse. He is already poetic about force. The missing piece is poetic about meaning. Asking him for that is asking the tool-self to include its antithesis. The book has argued that such inclusion would make him more whole, not less sharp. Whether a story ever grants that inclusion is not this book’s job to force.

### A practice that is not his, named as contrast

If someone wanted the why-floor without his aims, it would not look like mapping classmates. It would look like: take a moment that has no exam in it; do not ask what it is for; stay until a tone is exact — this is grief, or this is rest, or this is ugly, or this is enough. Exact tone is intuitive reason. Then return to tasks. The returning is how. The staying was why. He skips the staying. You do not have to.

The next chapters are about what dwelling would have to do with agency, and about the claim that thinking, at root, is one being — the dwelling-place he already has and barely lives in as a home.
