# Glossary

**Absorption.** The singular habit of taking events, people, and even inner remainders back into one project so that nothing stays truly outside.

**Animation.** The self-moving quality of a singular mind: it originates motion, expresses itself through situations, keeps going without being wound from outside, and has a tone. Not mood. The fact of inner motion.

**Antithesis / synthesis.** Used here modestly: a real contrary allowed into a field, then gathered into a thicker stance. Not a claim that Ayanokōji is a German philosopher.

**Bergson, Henri.** Philosopher of duration (*durée*), qualitative multiplicity, intellect versus intuition, and *élan vital*. Borrowed in this book for the difference between cutting the world into useful pieces and coinciding with a living flow.

**Contraction.** The tightening of attention and identity around a now, a fear, a role, a node. Useful for action. A prison if it claims to be the whole field.

**Contrapuntal thinking.** Working metaphor: two lines at once (his aim and another’s structures), still going forward. Limit: in him the second line is finally subordinate, unlike voices in a fugue that remain equal.

**Contextual multiplicity.** Many masks and readings, one source. Not the same as ontological or specific multiplicity, where others remain irreducible.

**Declaring / instantiating a truth.** Announcing your position as the room’s new reality. High resistance. His usual opposite: mapping.

**Deleuze / line of flight.** A break in a settled arrangement; a leak or leap. In singular logic, flights stay immanent — new modes of the one field, not a true outside.

**Duration (*durée*).** Lived time as interpenetrating tendencies, not clock beads. The now is a contraction of this thickness.

**Functional cycle.** Uexküll’s loop: a creature perceives a meaningful mark, then prescribes and enacts an effect that completes the meaning. Perception and action together make an Umwelt.

**Hallward, Peter.** Critic of Deleuzian thought who distinguishes **singular** individuation (one creative identity expressing itself) from **specific** individuation (many situated bodies in real relation). This book uses that contrast as a tool for reading Ayanokōji.

**How-floor / why-floor.** Two uses of intuition. How: exact coincidence with forces and timing, for operation. Why: exact coincidence with tone and meaning, poetic in nature, not “for what use?”

**Idiosyncrasy.** A small personal habit or quirk that often unguards a node because the person has not armored it.

**Image of thought.** How thinking presents itself to itself; the unspoken form of a mind. Not the contents (plans, memories) but the shape.

**Intellect.** For Bergson, the cutting, spatializing, practical faculty. Necessary. Incomplete if it pretends to be the whole of mind.

**Intuition.** Sympathetic coincidence with a moving reality. Not a vague guess. Can serve how or why.

**Intuitive reason.** Rigor in that coincidence: exact about flow, not vague about “energy.”

**Mapping.** Reading a person’s lived terrain — beliefs, walls, timing, idiosyncrasies — rather than flattening it with a speech.

**Mark (*Merkmal*).** In Uexküll, a sign that counts for a particular life. Not a neutral object.

**Medium.** The immanent field a singularity uses to differ from itself: memory, tendency, half-thoughts, available marks. Self-differing uses the medium to produce new information.

**Node.** A place where a person’s living mix is tightly contracted into a repeatable fear, hunger, pride, or story. A weather front, not a button.

**Poetic layer.** Meaning as qualitative unity of a motion, inhabited rather than used. Almost poetry as a form of truth, not as verse.

**Prescribing solutions semiotically.** The action-half of Uexküll’s cycle: through its own structures, the organism assigns what would count as answering a sign.

**Remainder.** What does not convert into use: a person still there after their function, a feeling that will not become data. Where the specific and the poetic knock.

**Self-differing.** The one field becoming-other without a second origin; unfolding novelty from its own medium.

**Self-positing origin.** The spark of singular animation: acting as real without waiting for the room to grant a role.

**Singular.** Organized around one inner identity or creative field. Variety is mode, not a parliament of equals.

**Specific.** Organized around many distinct, situated bodies whose relations do not fold into one creative identity. Politics and genuine otherness live here more easily.

**Surface / depth.** Surface: what presents as complete (the event, the smile, the rule). Depth: implied causation — beliefs, walls, debts, climate, prior moves.

**Tool-self.** A self trained and inhabited as an instrument: judged by results, adaptable in output, unified in origin. Ayanokōji’s image of thought.

**Umwelt.** The subjective meaningful world of a creature; perception-world plus effect-world. Not the full physical surroundings.

**Volition / agency.** Here: the capacity to assign the now a rank from a wider field, rather than letting the now dictate the whole of mind. Not total prediction. Not numbness.

**Wall.** The behavior that protects a node: joke, freeze, charm, attack, saintliness, intellect.

**White Room.** Fictional facility of his childhood; in this book, also a name for a medium that retunes a life to marks of performance and threat. Mentioned as origin, not as a training plan to copy.

**Why (poetic).** Inner necessity and tone of a movement; not motive-as-use.

### A note on sources

*Classroom of the Elite* (light novels and related adaptations) is the fictional home of Kiyotaka Ayanokōji. This book does not reproduce that text.

Philosophical names are used as tools, not as a claim that the character studies them. Primary touchstones: Bergson’s work on duration and intuition; Hallward’s singular/specific contrast; Deleuze’s language of immanence and flight; Uexküll’s *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans* (the functional cycle and Umwelt). Page numbers for Uexküll vary by edition; the cycle is developed in the early portion of the book, in the 2010 University of Minnesota translation around the 40s.

This manuscript is original analysis. It is not a method for controlling people.
