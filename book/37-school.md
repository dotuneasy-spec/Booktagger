# The School as Climate

Advanced Nurturing High School is not a neutral container. It is a climate machine that writes marks onto young Umwelten until students prescribe the solutions the machine prefers: compete, rank, ally, betray, smile for cameras.

### Institution as second White Room

The Room was closed, white, scored. The school is open, colorful, scored. He recognized the rhyme. Recognition is why he is not fooled by festivals and slogans. A festival is still a mark in a points-world. He can enjoy a surface of it as camouflage. He does not take the slogan as origin.

Other students need the slogans to house their pain. Slogans are fake resolutions at civilizational scale. He prefers settlements that hold. The school’s settlements often do not hold; they only look like order until the next special exam. He uses the gap between look and hold.

### Points as a semiotic prescription

Points are not money only. They are a sign that prescribes self-worth solutions. Students treat a number as a self. He treats a number as climate. That single difference is much of his agency. The now of a scoreboard is a tyrant for others. For him it is a mark in a longer cycle (stay unnamed, keep the class viable, do not become a demonstration).

Uexküll’s cycle, institutional version: the school presents marks (rank, camera, teacher’s eye); students prescribe (grind, freeze, bully); the cycle closes when the school’s effect is achieved (sorted humans). He inserts a different closure: look sorted, remain unsorted inside. That insertion is freedom-from. It is not yet a park.

### Class as fate-unit

By making a class a unit of fate, the school forces specific many-body life into a singular-looking team. Teams can be we or they-can-be-instruments. He treats Class D as a medium. The medium contains real people. The tragedy of the design is that a decent we and a useful instrument look alike from a points camera. He can build either. He defaults to instrument-with-we-surfaces, because surfaces hide him and instruments work.

If the school had graded kindness as kindness, his default might still be the same. The father-weather is older than the school. The school only rhymes.

### Why a climate-reader looks like a god

People who live inside a climate think the weather is nature. Someone who reads climate looks supernatural. He is not a god. He is literate in a language others take for air. Literacy is enough, in a contest, to look like fate. This book prefers literacy as the name. Fate is how you stop thinking.
