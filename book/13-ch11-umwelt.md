# Chapter 11

## The Umwelt: Perception and Meaning

Jakob von Uexküll was a biologist who refused the idea that animals live in our world minus a few senses. A tick, a dog, a human, a boy in a competitive school — each lives in an **Umwelt**: a subjective bubble of marks that mean something for that life. The physical surroundings (the *Umgebung*) are wider. The lived world is narrower and more meaningful.

This is the book the conversation reached for when it asked where “prescribing solutions semiotically” comes in. It comes in the second half of Uexküll’s **functional cycle**.

### The cycle, in plain words

A creature does not receive raw stuff and then, later, add meaning like sauce.

First, something is perceived as a **mark** (*Merkmal*). Not “butyric acid as chemistry,” for the tick, but “mammal-is-near” as a sign for a life that needs blood. Not “a smile as muscle,” for a student, but “I am safe / I am being hunted / I am being recruited.”

Then the creature does not merely twitch. It **prescribes an effect**. It assigns and enacts a response that completes the meaning of the sign: drop, bite; smile back; freeze; plot; walk away. The world of action (*Wirkwelt*) is the twin of the world of perception (*Merkwelt*). Together they are the Umwelt. The cycle closes when the creature finds, in the object, the signal of the effect it was seeking.

“Prescribing solutions semiotically” is not an extra. It is the action-half of meaning. The organism, through its own structures, decides what the sign is *for* and what would count as answering it.

In the 2010 English translation of *A Foray into the Worlds of Animals and Humans*, this loop is developed in the early stretch of the book, especially around the functional-cycle pages (roughly the 40s). The exact pagination shifts by edition. The idea does not: perception is already involvement; involvement already writes a solution-shape onto the scene.

### Ayanokōji’s Umwelt

His surroundings are a high school: corridors, exams, phones, uniforms, the sea, a city, a father far away and too close.

His Umwelt is not that list. His marks are: inconsistency, timing, who looks at whom, which rule is load-bearing, which emotion is performing, which silence is empty and which is full. He does not perceive most objects. He perceives contest-signs and freedom-signs. A vending machine is background unless it is a meeting place. A joke is background unless it is a wall. A tear is a mark: leak, performance, genuine contraction, opportunity, warning. He reads which.

This is “involving” in the sense the question meant. Perception is not a camera. It is a life selecting what counts, through the structures of that life. His structures were installed to select for control, prediction, anonymity, victory. Therefore his prescribed solutions tend to be: map, wait, nudge, hide, spend the minimum, absorb the result.

A kinder life would select other marks — the joke that is only a joke, the person as an end — and prescribe other solutions: stay, laugh, disclose. He can imitate that Umwelt. Imitation is a mask. The native bubble is the workshop.

### Why this matters for “surface and depth”

The surface is the mark as it first appears. Depth is the functional meaning in *his* cycle, and the guessed cycle of the other person.

When he “makes do with what he saw,” he is using a sparse set of marks, because that is all any Umwelt ever has. The tick has almost no world and still a complete one. He has a richer sensorium and still only a bubble. Necessity taught him to complete the cycle from incomplete marks: prescribe a next action that will generate better marks. Experiment is how an Umwelt grows without leaving itself.

He does not, as a rule, change perspective so fully that he lives in another’s bubble. He models other Umwelten as maps. Modeling is not inhabiting. Inhabiting would be intuition in Bergson’s strong sense, or love in a sense this character barely uses. He comes close, sometimes, which is why a few people disturb his cycle: they do not close the way objects should. They keep meaning something after the effect has been achieved. Remainder is how another Umwelt announces that it is not your tool.

### Semiotic prescription in a school

The school itself is a giant sign-machine. Points mean worth. Cameras mean being seen. Class rank means fate. Students prescribe solutions: grind, cheat, ally, bully, despair. Those solutions are not given by physics. They are written by a meaning-system onto young bodies.

He is fluent in that system and not devout. Fluency means he can use the signs. Lack of devotion means he can sometimes prescribe a solution the official language did not intend: lose on purpose, look stupid, let another take the crown. To devout students those moves look like noise. In his Umwelt they are elegant closures of a different cycle — the cycle of remaining unowned.

### Perception, volition, agency

If you are trapped in present context, your functional cycle has been hijacked by the loudest mark. The mark prescribes the solution automatically: reply, flinch, explain, scroll. Agency, in Uexküll’s vocabulary without his name, would be: noticing that the mark is a mark, not the world, and that another prescription is possible.

He does this as a habit. The insult arrives. The native prescription for a teenager is counter-insult or collapse. His prescription is: what does this mark mean in the longer cycle, and what effect would close *that* loop rather than the loud one?

That is not poetry yet. It is already a why of a practical kind: what is this for? The poetic why of a later chapter asks what the movement *is*, not only what it is for. Uexküll sits in the middle: meaning as use-for-a-life. Ayanokōji is a specialist of that middle. He is weaker at meaning as presence.

### Limits of the biological metaphor

A tick’s Umwelt is species-stable. A boy’s is historical. Training can retune marks. The White Room retuned him. The school tries to retune everyone. He is both the product of a retuning and someone who retunes others’ marks — teaching a classmate to see a threat where they saw a friend, or a tool where they saw a fate.

Retuning someone’s marks is the deepest version of “conditioning through their own structures.” You do not only give them a conclusion. You change what counts as a sign. After that, they will prescribe new solutions without you. That is the long game. It is also the most invasive. A person who has had their signs rewritten may not know they have been rewritten. They will call it growing up.

### Two Umwelten colliding

A school corridor is one physical space and dozens of bubbles. A popular student walks through a world of eyes. A frightened one walks through a world of edges and exits. He walks through a world of joints and cameras and ranks. They collide without knowing they are not in the same story. Conflict is often two prescriptions meeting: I need this mark to mean safety; you need it to mean dominance. He is good at conflict because he can model the other bubble well enough to predict the prescription. He still lives in his own. Modeling is not immigration.

Love, if it ever happens in a strong sense, would be a partial immigration: living, for stretches, in marks that are not contest-marks. A face as a face. He has not immigrated. He has visited, with a return ticket.

### Uexküll and the poetic why

Uexküll’s meaning is still biological-pragmatic: meaning for a life’s needs. The poetic why is meaning as tone, even when need is quiet. A tick has no poetic why that we can speak of. A boy might. The functional cycle explains his competence. It does not explain a remainder — the moment a mark means *more than* the effect he can prescribe. Music, a view of the sea, a person asleep, a memory that is not useful. If those occur, the Umwelt has a skylight. He keeps the skylight small. This book keeps pointing at it anyway.
