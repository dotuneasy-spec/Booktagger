# A Note on Influence, Ethics, and Fiction

This chapter exists because the conversation that made the book kept brushing a live wire: how to move other people.

Fiction is full of minds that move other people. Detectives, generals, con artists, teachers, lovers, gods. Describing those minds is older than this school story. The live wire is the moment description becomes a wish to do it, for real, to someone who did not ask.

Ayanokōji’s methods, as methods, are ethically empty. Conversation, mapping, waiting, letting someone say their own sentence — a good parent does these. A cult does these. A novelist does these to characters. The aim and the remainder decide the moral: did the other person remain an end. Did they get to refuse. Did they know they were in a piece of music that had a composer.

In the story, students did not consent to be in his composition. The school did not consent them into much of anything decent either. He is a parasite on an already parasitic institution. That makes him interesting. It does not make him a model.

This book’s rule, stated once in the open:

It will describe how a fictional mind sees joints, durations, marks, and plans.

It will not provide drills, scripts, or protocols for applying that seeing to unsuspecting people.

It will talk to *your* agency: your now, your ranking, your one being, your why.

If you notice that a chapter could be flipped into a manual, that is because structure is dual-use, like a knife. The coda is the handle-warning. Do not cut people and call it growth unless they are in a relationship of asked-for help, and even then, be suspicious of your love of their joints.

The White Room is a crime in the fiction. It is not a brand. Forgetting it as a training aesthetic was already a request in the conversation. The book honored that by keeping the Room as origin and wound, not as a gym.

If this note feels scolding, it is aimed at a real gravity in the source chat, not at the reader’s intelligence. Intelligence is what makes the gravity dangerous. He is intelligent. That is the point.
