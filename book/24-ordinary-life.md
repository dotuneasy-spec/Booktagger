# Ordinary Life

## The Wish the Workshop Cannot Score

Ayanokōji says, in various dry ways, that he wanted a normal life. Fans argue about whether he means it. The argument is usually too psychological: is he lying, is he curious, is he lonely.

There is a cleaner reading in the language of this book. Ordinary life is the name of a **flight** the tool-self can imagine but cannot, by itself, complete. It is a mode he unfolds — average clothes, average scores, average seat — without being able to land in the duration of the average. He can look ordinary. Looking is a surface. Living ordinary would be a different Umwelt: marks that are not contest-marks, prescriptions that are not victories.

### What “ordinary” means for a product

For a student who grew up in a noisy family, ordinary is default. For him, ordinary is exotic. It is a foreign country whose language is wasted time. Wasted time is how specific people become friends, get bored, invent jokes that are not signals. His training treated waste as error. So the wish for ordinary is a wish for error as a way of life.

That wish is already self-differing. The one being produces the thought of not being a tool. The thought is unstable. If it becomes a second origin, the singularity splits, which might be health. If it remains a cover, ordinary is only camouflage, which is the school-story most of the time.

He samples. Sampling is the experiment of making-do applied to a life. He tries a club, a meal, a conversation with no exam in it, a relationship with a function. He collects marks. He asks, without always wording it, whether this could be a home. Home would mean the now is allowed to be enough. He almost never grants that. The sample is filed. The workshop stays the house.

### Boredom

Ordinary life contains boredom that is not a tactic. Boredom without a tactic is a why-floor phenomenon: this stretch of time has a tone, and the tone is pale, and the paleness is not a problem to solve.

He is excellent at *using* boredom as cover. He is poor at *being* bored as a human among humans. Other students fill boredom with noise. He fills it with ranking, or with idle mapping, or with nothing that is still a throttle setting. Nothing-as-throttle is still a workshop state. Ordinary nothing is closer to play.

If he ever succeeds at ordinary, it will look, from inside, like a loss of sharpness. Friends will call it peace. The father would call it failure. The tool-self would feel a panic that has no contest to explain it. That panic would be the antithesis to “I am an instrument.” It would be worth staying with. He would be tempted to start a new exam in his head to make the panic useful. Use is how he flees the why.

### The sea, the city, the corridor

The story sometimes puts him in front of ordinary large things: water, a skyline, a hall at night. These are traditional poetic objects. A how-mind asks what they are for (meeting place, calm the body, wait). A why-mind lets them be tone.

He can feel tone. Feeling is not the same as dwelling. A one-second exactness — this is empty, this is wide — is the poetic layer as a flash. Then the functional cycle closes: and therefore I will… The “and therefore” is the trained reflex. Ordinary life would be the ability to have the flash without the therefore.

This is not mysticism. It is a Saturday.

### Can a tool choose rest?

Rest, for him, is often recovery of throttle, which is still in service of later work. Rest as an end would be a specific, almost animal, fact: the body has a now that is not a mark for a contest. He has a body. He uses it. Using a body is not the same as being one among others in a park.

The wish for ordinary is, at bottom, a wish to be a body in a park without a father and without a scheme. The book cannot give him that park. It can say that the wish is the most important sentence the tool-self has ever unfolded from its medium, and that he keeps translating the sentence back into camouflage, which is how a workshop survives a prayer.

### Ordinary people are not failed Ayanokōjis

A nasty implication of his legend is that scattered, feeling, present-trapped people are simply untrained.

This book rejects that. Specific, messy, now-soaked life is not a bug. It is one of the ways meaning happens. A parent who drops the ranking to pick up a crying child is not less than a strategist. They are on a different floor, sometimes a better one. His superpower is a deformation that won a certain game. Ordinary is not the easy mode of that game. Ordinary is another game whose scoring is love, waste, and remaining human when no one is grading.

He wanted that game without knowing how to play it. That is not a plot hole. That is the character.
