## 2. How FarmPark operates (their public model)

This is reconstructed from **their** website, LinkedIn About, founder CV, 2021–22 jobs, and May 2023 LinkedIn articles. It is not a signed SOP. Where they are silent, the file says so.

### 2.1 What they sell — three products, one loop

FarmPark’s services page and home page repeat the same three offers:

| Product | What they say they do | What that means in practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Farmland acquisition** | “Secure prime agricultural land through our streamlined purchasing process.” | They source and sell (or broker) land. Client-services staff take investors on **inspection / site tours**. |
| **Co-investment** | “Partner with experienced investors… share in the profits”; “share risks and rewards.” | Client money goes into a managed farm; returns are split. This is their default commercial shape. |
| **Expert farm management** | “Professional farming oversight that optimizes crop yields and operational efficiency.” | They (or a farmer they place) run day-to-day operations so the client does not have to farm. |

LinkedIn About, in one sentence: they **select agricultural land for investors, cultivate it, and generate income for those investors from whichever produce the investor wants** — crop or ranch.

That last clause is the only public hook for tomato: tomato is allowed as **client-chosen produce**, not as a FarmPark advertised specialty. Public posts that name a sector name **catfish, poultry and hatchery in Lagos and Ibadan**, not open-field hybrid tomato into Mile 12.

### 2.2 FARM-IN / FARM-OUT / e-Farming

David Omaghomi describes FarmPark as agri-tech / agri-business **farm estates** with three named packages that “connect urban people and rural dwellers”:

| Package (their label) | Reading from their own language | Fit for SNLB tomato |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **FARM-IN** | Urban / diaspora / professional capital **enters** a farm project (minimum capital, ROI, “invest without managing it yourself”). | SNLB is already in. Do not farm-in as their investor. |
| **FARM-OUT** | They **operate** the land (or place rural operators) so the capital provider is not on the hoe. “Do you train or manage farms?” — they offer both. | This is the only package that can become an FMC contract — if rewritten as **services on SNLB’s land**, not an equity farm-out. |
| **e-Farming** | Track the project **from anywhere** (diaspora, busy professionals). | Useful as reporting (photos, weekly pack). Not a substitute for an Ogun farm manager. |

They also invite clients to **“join existing farm projects”** and ask “which sector is most profitable right now?” — a product-picker model, not a locked tomato calendar.

### 2.3 The operating loop they describe

From LinkedIn posts, the Farm Officer JD (April 2022) and the May 2023 articles *Farm Management: Tips for running an effective Farm* and *Features of a Typical Farm in Lagos*:

1. **Intake.** Client asks: minimum capital; train vs manage; fish vs crop ROI; full setup; diaspora tracking. Sales / client services close the deal after a **site tour**.
2. **Land.** They want arable land, water, proximity to market, size matched to the crop. They will sell land or use an existing farm. Location is a first-order decision in their Lagos farm-opportunity article.
3. **Plan and budget.** Crop (or livestock) selection, planting and harvest **schedules**, irrigation plan, pest and disease measures, marketing, weather and soil. They treat a written business plan as the execution tool.
4. **Place a farmer on the ground.** “A typical farmer in Lagos is said to have no day off.” Equipment from hoe/cutlass up to tractor, plow, sprayer, planter. Storage building. Logistics to move produce to sale.
5. **Supervise resources.** Water, fertilizer, land, energy; irrigation scheduling; soil tests for nutrient need.
6. **Data / tech.** Collect data on nutrients, crop growth, livestock health, topology — stated as a principle, with **no named farm-management app** in public materials.
7. **Risk.** Crop insurance, **diversify crops and livestock**, stay current on sector trends. Diversification is their stated risk tool — it fights SNLB’s tomato-only block.
8. **Harvest and remit.** Cultivate, generate income, investor receives returns. Public material does **not** name Mile 12, a selling desk, or a basket spec.
9. **Report remotely.** e-Farming / “track from anywhere.”

The 2022 Farm Officer was hired to: **analyse existing operations, crops, poultry, staff and financial documents; recommend improvements; prepare planting and harvest schedules.** That is a mixed crop-and-poultry supervisor, not a 100 ha tomato drip superintendent.

### 2.4 What they say a working farm must have

Their “typical farm” checklist (useful as a visit scorecard):

- Arable land, sized and sited for the crop (and, in their world, for animals).
- A farmer on site every day.
- Tools and, at scale, tractor / plow / sprayer / planter.
- A building for storage (shed, barn, poultry house, silo — their examples are mixed livestock/crop).
- Logistics to move produce to the house, the market, or a processor.

That list is generic. It does not specify drip laterals, fertigation tanks, staking at 22–25k plants/ha, or a 1.5-hour Mile 12 run.

### 3.3 Tomato-specific gaps in how they operate

Their published method is **plan → place a farmer → irrigate and spray → harvest → remit**. For this tomato plan that is not enough until they add, in writing:

1. A **hybrid cultivar** and plant population (study: 22–25k plants/ha, drip from transplant one).
2. A **two-cycle calendar** that starts Regular (not “whichever sector is hottest”).
3. A **Peak *Tuta* / rain SOP** — their articles mention pests in general and livestock diversification as the hedge.
4. A **Mile 12 grade-and-sell SOP** (firm fruit, crates, pre-auction, buyer log). They do not name that market.
5. **Tomato-only** on the master block — their risk article tells farmers to diversify into livestock.
6. **Fee as naira retainer / SLA**, not a share of harvest proceeds.
7. Named **Ogun** staffing, not a Lekki desk plus Ibadan fish crews.

If they cannot show a tomato or intensive-vegetable cycle you can walk, keep them as **land/admin advisor** and hire the agronomist yourself. Do not let a poultry-and-fish operator silently become the tomato superintendent.

## 8. Bottom line

FarmPark is a **real Lekki office** (King’s Deck, Chevron) that has, since about 2017–18, sold **land, co-investment and managed-farm returns**. Their operating method is: take a client, pick or accept a produce, put a farmer on land, irrigate and schedule, harvest, remit. Public crop talk is **fish, poultry and hatchery**. Tomato is only in scope as “whichever produce the investor desires.”

That method can support SNLB **if** it is rewritten as **advisory / services on SNLB’s Ogun tomato block**, with SNLB keeping land, ₦8 million, 50/50 and Mile 12. It cannot support SNLB if they farm-in your capital, farm-out equity in the 100 ha, add livestock, or sell the fruit.

Treat the first meeting as due diligence. Get the CAC entity, a Cycle 1 advisory letter that matches the RACI in §3.1, and a farm you can walk. Close the ticks in section 4 before you pay a retainer or name them as operator in a credit file.
