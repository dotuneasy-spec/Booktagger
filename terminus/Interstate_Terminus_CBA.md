# Private interstate coach terminus
# Cost–Benefit Analysis

Landlord model — rent the station, do not buy the buses  
Nigeria — one highway-edge site (Lagos–Ogun or equivalent) first

A private **state-to-state** terminus, open every day, sized so **morning, afternoon and night waves** reuse the same bays. Private fleets (GUO-class, mid-tier, new CNG lines) rent gates and layover. The owner takes **rent plus the station economy** (food, shops, parking, ads) — not the ticket, and not a 10–25% “loading tax” of the old motor park.

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Prepared | August 2026 |
| Horizon | 10 years unlevered, residual at year 10 |
| Currency | Nigerian naira (₦), nominal |
| Planning site | ~2.0 ha, highway-edge Lagos–Ogun (not Jibowu) |
| Planning capital | **₦2.57 billion** |
| Discount rates | 22% (venture / high-risk); 18% and 15% (property) |
| Tax | 32.1% of PBT (30% CIT + 3% education tax) |
| Classification | Illustrative planning note — not investment advice |

**Verdict in one line.** The **shape** of the business is right (airport, not airline). On **bought** outer-Lagos land with 72% bay occupancy it **earns** (Y3 NPAT ~₦310m, IRR ~16%, payback ~7 years) but it **does not clear a 22% hurdle**. It **does** clear a ~15% property hurdle. It **fails** if you overpay for inner-city land or open empty. It **works well** if land is a **concession** and bays are **pre-let**. The **36 states** are on the **destination board**, not in the CapEx as 36 stations.

## 1. The model

### 1.1 Who pays whom

| You own | Tenant | You do **not** take |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Land (or long concession), pavement, building, clock | Private interstate operators | The passenger ticket |
| Night power, security, toilets, lounges | Retail / food / ads concessionaires | A tout-style % of every fare |
| Optional later: CNG, wash, lodge | Park-and-ride / drop-off cars | The buses |

**Product.** Contracted **bays** (monthly) + **casual slots** for overflow + **overnight layover** + **retail GLA** + parking + ads. Same 36 load bays work three times a day if the timetable is real.

**Not this product.** A LAMATA BRT terminal. A NURTW open park. A Jibowu-street of single-brand compounds you hope GIGM will abandon. A fleet.

### 1.2 Why “different times of day” is the economics

Sizing the yard for every operator’s fleet at 07:00 is how you bankrupt a station. Sizing for the **fattest wave** (often night + weekend) and **reusing bays** is how ₦2.6bn of concrete can earn. Night operations are not optional: that is a large part of interstate money.

### 1.3 What must be true before earthworks

1. Highway exit — a loaded coach must reach the express.  
2. Written designation as an interstate load point (state / LASTMA / LGA).  
3. **Signed slot contracts** covering at least **~40% of bays** (about 15 of 36 on planning).  
4. 05:00–24:00 staffing and power.  
5. You are the **Lagos (or Abuja) end**. Operators already have the other city.

If (3) fails, do not build.

### 1.4 What “state-to-state” includes — and what it does not

**The 36 states are destinations, not 36 construction sites.** Coaches already leave Lagos for the federation (East, Abuja, PH, Kano, Calabar, the West). One designated Lagos-end station **serves those routes**. That is how the 36 states sit in this CBA: **as demand on the timetable**, not as 36 copies of the ₦2.57bn yard.

**Not in v1.0 of the numbers:** 36 state governments as counterparties; a terminus in every capital; FCT as a second model; population-weighted demand by state. Those are Section 6.

## 2. What it costs

### 2.1 Planning-case capital (₦ million)

Highway-edge Lagos–Ogun, 20,000 m², 36 load bays, 2,500 m² terminal building, solar + generator, not a 10 ha mega-terminal.

| Item | ₦ million |
| --- | --- |
| Land (20,000 m² × ₦38,000) | 760 |
| Paving / yard (16,000 m² × ₦24,000) | 384 |
| Terminal building (2,500 m² × ₦300,000) | 750 |
| Utilities (power, water, drainage, lighting, CCTV, fence/gates) | 260 |
| Fit-out (lounges, toilets, offices) | 120 |
| Design, preliminaries, contingency (14% of hard works) | 212 |
| **Works subtotal** | **1,726** |
| Pre-open working capital | 80 |
| **Total uses** | **2,566** |

Build rates sit in 2026 warehouse / industrial bands (about ₦180k–₦350k/m² for buildings; paving lower). **Land is the swing factor.** ₦38,000/m² is an **outer corridor** price, not Jibowu and not Lekki Free Zone ( serviced industrial land there has been listed around **$250/m²**). If the site is inner Lagos, use the **stress** column.

| Case | Land idea | Total CapEx |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stress** | Inner-corridor freehold (~₦80,000/m², 1.8 ha) | **₦3.74bn** |
| **Planning** | Highway-edge Lagos–Ogun | **₦2.57bn** |
| **Upside** | Long public/private **concession** (little freehold) | **₦2.01bn** |

Works depreciate over 20 years. Land is not depreciated. No debt in the base run.

### 2.2 Annual operating cost (planning, full run-rate)

About **₦418 million** a year at full occupancy: staff, contracted security, diesel/solar, maintenance (~2.8% of works), insurance (~1.5% of assets), LGA/rates/signage, lean HQ. Roughly half of that is **sticky** if buses stay away — which is why empty stations die.

## 3. What it returns

### 3.1 Planning steady revenue (year 3 onward)

72% of 36 bays contracted (**~26 bays**) at ₦1.8m per bay per month. Casual slots and layover on top. Retail is **lease**, not a cut of fare.

| Line | ₦ million / year | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contracted bays | 560 | 58% |
| Casual / overflow departures | 178 | 19% |
| Overnight layover | 65 | 7% |
| Retail / food leases | 97 | 10% |
| Parking / drop-off | 36 | 4% |
| Advertising | 24 | 3% |
| **Gross** | **960** | 100% |

Ramp: year 1 = 55% of that, year 2 = 80%, year 3+ = 100%. That is the “open empty” tax.

### 3.2 Planning P&L (₦ million)

| | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 (run-rate) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gross | 528 | 768 | 960 |
| Opex | 339 | 383 | 418 |
| **EBITDA** | **189** | **385** | **542** |
| EBITDA margin | 36% | 50% | **56%** |
| Depreciation | 86 | 86 | 86 |
| Tax | — | — | 146 |
| **NPAT** | **70** | **203** | **310** |
| NPAT margin | 13% | 26% | **32%** |
| Unlevered FCF | 156 | 289 | 396 |

Y3 cash yield on ₦2.57bn: **EBITDA / capital ≈ 21%**. That is a property yield, not a kiosk fee stack.

### 3.3 Three cases (unlevered, 10 years, residual at Y10)

| | Stress | Planning | Upside |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CapEx | ₦3.74bn | ₦2.57bn | ₦2.01bn |
| Bay occupancy | 48% | 72% | 86% |
| Steady gross | ₦378m | ₦960m | ₦1.76bn |
| Y3 EBITDA | **−₦183m** | **₦542m** | **₦1.34bn** |
| Y3 NPAT | −₦292m | **₦310m** | **₦852m** |
| Unlevered IRR | **−9%** | **16%** | **43%** |
| Payback | Never in 10y | **~7.4 years** | **~2.9 years** |
| NPV @ 22% | **−₦4.17bn** | **−₦0.71bn** | **+₦2.61bn** |
| NPV @ 18% | −₦4.13bn | **−₦0.27bn** | +₦3.71bn |
| NPV @ 15% | −₦4.08bn | **+₦0.16bn** | +₦4.79bn |

**How to read this.** At 22% (harsh, venture-style) planning is a **no**. At 15% (plausible for leased infrastructure with residual land) planning is a **thin yes**. Upside is “do not buy the dirt, concession it, and fill the bays.” Stress is “Jibowu prices, half-empty station” — a hole.

EBITDA break-even occupancy (volume lines scale together): about **23% of bays** on planning cost stack (~8 contracted bays) — **if** you actually cut variable cost. In stress, opex and land are high enough that the station **does not break even** at any occupancy in the scan: the building is too dear for the rent.

## 4. Cost–benefit

### 4.1 Monetised (planning)

| | 10-year view |
| --- | --- |
| **Costs (present, t=0)** | ₦2.57bn cash out |
| **Benefits** | Operating FCF Y1–Y9 + Y10 FCF including residual (~₦4.2bn book residual in the model: mix of capitalised EBITDA and land) |
| **NPV @ 22%** | **−₦0.71bn** |
| **NPV @ 15%** | **+₦0.16bn** |
| **IRR** | **16%** |

You are paid for: (1) gates that private BRT/interstate owners will rent because LASTMA wants designated loading and they cannot all afford a Jibowu compound; (2) night waves on the same slab; (3) shops and parking that follow passengers; (4) land that can still be land in year 10.

You pay: a large, illiquid site; political designation; union pressure; diesel; the risk that GIGM-class brands never leave their own lounge.

### 4.2 Benefit / cost (undiscounted cash, crude)

Ten years of operating FCF before residual is about ₦156 + 289 + 8 × 396 ≈ **₦3.61bn**, plus residual **₦4.16bn** at Y10, against ₦2.57bn in. That looks fat **because residual is large**. Haircut residual to land-only (~₦1.5bn at 7% appreciation on ₦760m) and the story is a **16%-class yield**, not a double-your-money venture. Do not underwrite the residual as if it were cash in year 3.

### 4.3 Sensitivity (direction)

| If this happens | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Land is a **concession** (upside land line) | NPV @ 22% flips **positive** |
| Inner-Lagos freehold (stress land) | Value destroyed even if buses come |
| Pre-lets fail; occupancy stays ~50% | Stress path — do not open |
| Night shift cancelled | Lose layover + a large share of casual; IRR drops hard |
| You take 15% of fares like an old park | Operators stay at Jibowu; “benefit” is imaginary |
| CNG / workshop later | Extra yield not in the base case |

## 5. Versus owning the buses

Same order of capital: **25 coaches × ₦95m + ₦280m float ≈ ₦2.66bn**.

| | Terminus (planning Y3) | Fleet (illustrative, every year) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you are | Airport | Airline |
| Gross | ₦0.96bn | ~₦12.3bn |
| EBITDA | ₦0.54bn (56% margin) | ~₦1.36bn (**11%** margin in this sketch) |
| NPAT | ₦0.31bn | ~₦0.72bn |
| What can wipe it | Empty bays, land price | Fuel, crash, empty return, FX parts, fare war |
| Residual | Land + a going station | Used iron, eight-year life |
| Everyday / everyone | The **place** is used every day by many brands | Only **your** seats |

Paper NPAT is **higher on the fleet**. Quality of earnings, residual, and “piece of whatever comes to the gate” are **better on the station**. Your instinct stands: **do not buy the buses** if the goal is a large, durable take on a daily public flow. The fleet sketch is also **fragile** — 11% EBITDA is a good year, not a covenant.

Do not take a % of their ticket as the core rent. That is how parks get replaced by private compounds. Charge **bays and clock**; own **shops**.

## 6. The 36 states

**Short answer.** No — the cash model is **one site**. Yes — the *traffic* is already **state-to-state across the federation**. Mixing those two is how you either under-claim (only Lagos) or over-build (36 monuments).

### 6.1 Three different “involve the 36 states”

| Meaning | In this CBA? | What it actually is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Destinations** — buses from *this* station run to many states, every day | **Yes, as the product.** Occupancy assumes a mix of East / Abuja / South / North legs, not a Lagos–Ibadan shuttle | One Lagos (or Abuja) **hub**. Operators hold the other end |
| **B. 36 governments** — each state concessions you land | **No.** Only **Lagos or Ogun** (the siting state) is the Phase 1 counterparty | A second state appears when you build the **pair** (e.g. Abuja) |
| **C. 36 termini** — copy the yard in every capital | **No, and should not be.** 36 × ₦2.57bn ≈ **₦92bn** of concrete. Most capitals cannot fill 36 bays at ₦1.8m/month | Hubs, not a flag in every state |

### 6.2 Why 36 copies fail the same test this CBA already ran

Stress is “expensive land + empty bays.” That is the default in a thin capital. A station in a state with little *originating* interstate luxury traffic is the stress case **before** you pour. Daily, everyone-uses-it volume in this country **concentrates**: Lagos as origin, then Abuja, Port Harcourt, Kano, Onitsha/Enugu, Ibadan. The other states are mostly **where the coach is going**, not where you need a 2 ha private ISBT on day one.

### 6.3 A federation-scale map that can still be true

| Phase | Where | Role of the 36 states |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | One Lagos–Ogun (or Abuja) hub | **All 36 as possible destinations** on tenant routes. One siting government |
| **2** | Pair: Lagos **and** Abuja (or PH) | Two ends of the fattest legs. Two governments, not 36 |
| **3** | 5–8 **hubs** (add Kano, Enugu/Onitsha, Ibadan, PH if not already) | Covers the corridors that actually run every day. Remaining states stay **destinations** |
| **Not a phase** | One monument per state capital | Political map, not a P&L |

Order-of-magnitude if Phase 3 is earned (illustrative, not a second model): six hubs at ~₦1.8–2.6bn each is about **₦12–15bn**, not ₦92bn. Still only after Phase 1 bays are actually full.

### 6.4 What to do with state governments

- **Siting state (Lagos or Ogun):** designation, LASTMA, land or concession — this is the only government that can kill or make Phase 1.  
- **Destination states:** you do not need their C of O to sell a Lagos bay to a bus going to Enugu. The **operator** already has (or rents) a park there.  
- **“Involve 36 states” as a tour of 36 governors:** that is a political campaign, not occupancy. Do not spend the ₦2.57bn on it.

## 7. What the numbers do not capture

**Benefits (real, not in NPV)**  
Everyday infrastructure; many operators under one roof; a file of fleets you could later sell energy or wash to; possible BITP/LAMATA overflow if city terminals clog; inclusion of mid-tier lines that cannot buy Jibowu.

**Costs (real, not fully costed)**  
Union/tout pressure; designation delayed a year (burns the ramp); LASTMA relocates interstate loading to a state box (Oshodi-class) and your highway site is stranded; flood / ROW; you still need the **other city’s** park in the operator’s network; GIGM already runs tens of **own** lounges — they are not your core tenant.

## 8. Go / no-go

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Is landlord-not-fleet the right shape? | **Yes.** |
| Build on **bought** outer-Lagos land, no pre-lets, 22% hurdle? | **No.** |
| Build if land is **concession or cheap edge**, **≥40% bays pre-let**, you accept **~15–16% IRR** as property? | **Go — Phase 0.** |
| Inner-city trophy site, Jibowu prices? | **No.** |
| Own 25 coaches instead, same cheque? | **No** if you want residual and sleep. Yes only if you *want* to be a bus company. |
| Build **36** state-capital copies to “cover Nigeria”? | **No.** Cover the federation as **destinations** from hubs. |

**Phase 0 (before ₦2.6bn):** 90 days. Site longlist with express access. Counsel on designation. Term sheets from **four operators**, **≥15 bays**, routes that already touch many states. Only then buy or concession. Order of spend: **paper, then dirt.**

**Capital this year if gates pass:** about **₦2.6bn** planning (or ~₦2.0bn if the state holds the land). Do not order a second **city** until year 3 occupancy holds. Do not order 36.

## 9. Appendices

### A. Assumptions (planning)

- 36 load bays; 72% contracted; ₦1.8m/bay/month  
- Casual: 12 extra departures/day × ₦45,000 × 330 days  
- Layover: 18 buses × ₦12,000 × 300 nights  
- Retail: 900 m² × 75% occupancy × ₦12,000/m²/month  
- Hours that match interstate reality, including night  
- Ramp 55% / 80% / 100%  
- Residual at Y10: 70% of Y10 EBITDA capitalised at 11% yield + 30% appreciated land and remaining works book  
- Rebuild: `python3 model/cba_model.py`

### B. Informal park comparison

Some open parks still take **10–25% of the fare** as a loading fee. That can look richer than ₦1.8m/bay. It is also why serious brands built **private compounds**. This CBA prices a **contracted station** cheap enough versus that tax that operators will come, and rich enough in **retail** that you do not need their ticket.
